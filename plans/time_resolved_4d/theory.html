1. Theory

This blueprint defines what the time-resolved reconstruction computes: the quantities, the data term, the motion model, the temporal prior, and the iteration. The notation follows the Theory page of the mbirtorch documentation.

2. Time-resolved data term

Each view shows the object at the moment the view was taken. The data term therefore compares each view with the object at that moment. In contrast, MACE4D now compares each frame with every view in an angular window, as if the object stood still during the window.

The reconstruction holds K frames. Frame xk is the estimate of the object at time tk. At a time τ between two frame times, the object is a weighted average of those two frames. The data term adds up the mismatch between each view and the object at the time of that view.

x(τ) =∑k=0K−1φk(τ)xk f(x) =12σy2∑j=0J−1‖yj−Ajx(τj)‖Λj2

Here φk is the hat function of frame k. It equals 1 at time tk and falls linearly to 0 at the neighboring frame times. The hat functions of the first and last frames are one-sided. The frame times are equally spaced, with F frames in each rotation of V views.

tk =τ0+kVF,k=0,…,K−1 K =⌊F(τJ−1−τ0)V⌋+1

View j therefore involves only two frames, and f is a sum of one term per interval between frame times, f=∑ifi(xi,xi+1). Interval i holds the views with ti≤τj<ti+1, and the last interval also holds a view at time tK−1. The views after time tK−1 are dropped, as MACE4D drops the views that cannot fill a frame.

The hat functions introduce motion blur into the data term. Between two frame times, the model of the object is a weighted average of the two frames. A moving edge therefore appears in the model as a blend of its positions in the two frames, instead of at its position at time τ. The blur is small only when an edge moves about one voxel or less from one frame to the next, so faster motion needs more frames per rotation. More complex forms of the interpolation could be proposed later. For example, each of the two frames could first be moved by the motion model to time τ, and the two moved frames then averaged.

3. Motion model: rigid parts plus springs

The object is made of a static part, moving parts that translate without rotating, and springs. Each spring joins the static part to one moving part. The label volume assigns each voxel of frame 0 to one of these parts. The label of a spring covers its solid envelope, including the gaps between its coils. The displacement of voxel r from frame 0 to frame k is the following.

Dk(r)= { 0,if voxel r is in the static part dm,k,if voxel r is in moving part m uc(r)dm,k,if voxel r is in spring c, which joins moving part m

Spring c has its fixed end at ac, the center of its contact with the static part. Its unit axis ec points from ac to the center of its contact with moving part m, and ℓc is the distance between the two centers in frame 0. The spring coordinate uc(r) is the following fraction, clipped to the interval from 0 to 1.

uc(r)=(r−ac)⋅ecℓc

A spring stretches or compresses when its moving part moves along its axis, and it shears when the part moves sideways. The motion in frame k is described by three numbers for each moving part. A dense displacement field would need three numbers for every voxel.

4. Motion-compensated temporal prior

The temporal prior penalizes the change from each frame to the next after the motion between them is removed. It has the form of the qGGMRF prior. Each of its pairs links voxel r of frame k+1 to the point of frame k that moves to voxel r.

ht(x;d) =∑k=0K−2∑r∉Skbtρ(xk+1,r−[Mkxk]r) [Mkxk]r =xk(r−wk+1(r)(dm,k+1−dm,k))

The operator Mk is computed from d. It shifts moving part m by dm,k+1−dm,k, it shifts each point of a spring by a fraction of that difference, and it leaves the static part in place. Here m is the moving part that holds voxel r, or the moving part that its spring joins. The weight wk+1(r) depends on the label of voxel r in frame k+1.

wk+1(r)= { 0,if voxel r is in the static part 1,if voxel r is in moving part m (r−ac)⋅ecℓc+dm,k+1⋅ec,if voxel r is in spring c

The spring weight is clipped to the interval from 0 to 1. Its denominator is the length of the spring in frame k+1, which must stay positive. The labels of frame k+1 are the labels of frame 0 moved by Dk+1. Where moved labels overlap, a moving part takes precedence over a spring, and both take precedence over the static part. A voxel that no moved label covers is static. Frame xk is evaluated between voxels by cubic B-spline interpolation.

The set Sk holds the voxels that the sum skips. A voxel is in Sk when its label in frame k+1 differs from the label of its source point in frame k, as where a moving part or a spring uncovers the background. A voxel is also in Sk when it lies within one voxel of the boundary of a moving part or a spring in frame k+1. The potential ρ is the qGGMRF potential, with the parameters σx, p, q, and T of the spatial prior. The weight bt applies to each temporal pair.

5. Iteration

For fixed d, the frames minimize the following cost. Here h is the qGGMRF prior of the mbirtorch documentation, applied to each frame as a 3D volume.

x^=argminx{f(x)+∑k=0K−1h(xk)+ht(x;d)}

MACE computes this minimizer as the consensus of five agents: the data-fit agents for the even and the odd intervals, the qGGMRF denoiser applied to each frame, and the temporal agents for the even and the odd pairs of frames. The temporal agent for the even pairs holds the terms of ht with even k, each of which links frames k and k+1. The temporal agent for the odd pairs holds the terms with odd k. The intervals or pairs within one agent share no frame, so each data-fit agent and each temporal agent solves independent two-frame problems. The agents use one step size and equal weights.

An outer loop alternates this reconstruction with an estimate of d. The starting d comes from registering each moving part of each MACE4D frame to the same part in MACE4D frame 0, on which the labels are drawn. That d is interpolated linearly in time to the frame times tk. It is held at 0 before the time of MACE4D frame 0, and at its last value after the time of the last MACE4D frame. The first MACE pass starts from the MACE4D frames, interpolated in time in the same way.

The temporal weight starts at a fraction of bt and rises to bt over the outer iterations. Once it reaches bt, the loop stops when no translation changes by more than ε voxels from one estimate to the next, or after N outer iterations. The result is the frames of the last MACE pass and the d that pass used.

The motion estimate registers each moving part of each frame to a template. The template x¯ is the mean of all frames, each moved back to frame 0 by the current d.

x¯r =1K∑k=0K−1xk(r+dm,k),r∈part m dm,k ←argminδ∑r∈part mρ(xk(r+δ)−x¯r)

Here xk(r+δ) is frame k interpolated by cubic B-splines at the position of voxel r shifted by δ. The minimization is not convex, so a local search starts from the current dm,k.
