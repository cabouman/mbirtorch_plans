## Units and terms

An offset is given in channels, which are detector pixels along the channel axis.  A rotation is
given in degrees.  The edge displacement of a rotation is the distance it moves the edge pixel
of the detector, in pixels.  A detector's size is written out, as in a 512-channel detector, so
a bare number of channels is always an offset.  A roll test rolls the sinogram by a whole number
of channels with `np.roll` and requires the offset estimate to move by that number.

The module is `mbirtorch/preprocess/geometry_calibration.py`.  Its entry points under test are
the offset estimator (`estimate_det_channel_offset`), the rotation estimator
(`estimate_det_rotation`), and the direction check (`check_rotation_direction`).  The bilinear
kernel is `_rotation_kernel` in `mbirtorch/preprocess/utilities.py`, and it is the resampling
that `correct_det_rotation`, `apply_calibration`, `reduce_sinogram`, and `scan_to_sino` all use.
The artifact phantom is the NSI object of the real-scan jobs, scanned without and with a metal
insert.  The far-slice job is `real_scan_rotation_recon.py`, which reconstructed slices far from
the central plane at four fixed rotations and judged them by sharpness after a blur.

## How the synthetic examples were made

The offset examples are independent of the code under test, and every rotation example passes
through the module's own rotation code.  That is the answer to Greg's doubt.  It comes from an
inventory of the tests, the demo, and every experiment script, and each site quoted below was
re-read in this session.  The two halves need different judgments, so they are taken in turn.

Every synthetic offset was injected through the projector.  A generating model with the true
offset forward projected the phantom, and the estimating model started from zero
(`conjugate_offset_recovery.py:100`, `calibration_512_gautschi.py:139-140`,
`residual_score_probe.py:209`, `tests/test_geometry_calibration.py:560`).  The real-scan test
shifted the data with `np.roll`, which moves whole channels and interpolates nothing
(`real_scan_validation.py:344`).  The estimator itself applies a candidate offset as an exact
Fourier phase shift of the mirrored opposite views (`geometry_calibration.py:1065`).  The
generation, the correction, and the estimation therefore use three different mechanisms.  What
they still share is the projector's geometry conventions, which is the inverse crime the plan
names.  Two checks test that shared part.  The roll test moves real data with no model at all.
The vendor comparisons supply values the package did not compute.

Every synthetic rotation was applied by resampling, because the projectors have no
detector-rotation parameter.  The examples fall into three groups.  The two accuracy
experiments generated the sinogram at four times the detector resolution with the projector,
then rotated and binned it in one `reduce_sinogram` call, whose rotation is the bilinear kernel
(`rotation_interpolation_bias.py:68-79`, `calibration_512_gautschi.py:166-174`,
`geometry_calibration.py:443`).  The gate tests, the demo, and the figure script rotated at the
detector's own resolution with `correct_det_rotation` (`tests/test_geometry_calibration.py:808`,
`demo/demo_10_geometry_calibration.py:62`, `estimators_in_action.py:50`).  The added-rotation
job on real scans used `correct_det_rotation` as well (`real_scan_rotation_check.py:283`).  The
four-times sampling shrinks the generation's smoothing to about a quarter of what the
estimator's own resampling applies, and whether four times is enough was not checked
(`rotation_interpolation_bias.md`).  The four-times sampling changes nothing about the shared
convention, because the rotation code is the same.

What the rotation recoveries prove is therefore specific.  The estimator scores each candidate
with a different implementation, OpenCV's cubic `warpAffine` (`geometry_calibration.py:1313`),
so the recoveries cross two implementations and are not circular in the interpolation.
`rotation_kernel_conventions.md`, a new measurement this session, compared the conventions.  The
bilinear kernel and OpenCV's bilinear rotation about the same center compute the same map to
6.2e-07.  A sign error between them would show at 1.5e-02 or more, and a half-channel center
error at 1.3e-04 or more.  The two production paths themselves, bilinear against cubic, agree
only to 2.3e-03 to 3.7e-03, which is larger than the center control.  That the two paths rotate
about the same center therefore rests on their source, where the two formulas name the same
point (`geometry_calibration.py:1309`, `utilities.py:234-236`).  A sub-channel center
disagreement would act as a small channel shift, and the offset estimate absorbs such a shift
in the three-step sequence.  The scale of the convention is verified only in part.  The cluster
recoveries are within 0.5 percent of the angle from 1.34 pixels of edge displacement upward,
the interpolation bias below one pixel is 10 to 24 percent, and the gate tests tolerate 12
percent (`calibration_512_gautschi.md`, `tests/test_geometry_calibration.py:813`).  One
independent control covers the generation itself.  LEAP's `estimate_tilt`, run on the same
generated sinograms, errs 10 to 18 percent high where the module errs 10 to 24 percent low
(`calibration_512_gautschi.md`).  Two estimators biased in opposite directions on one dataset
point at each estimator's resampling and not at the way the data were made
(`increment_2_findings.md`).

The real scans supplied what the synthetic examples could not.  The NSI scans carry a physical
tilt, and the jobs held the vendor's recorded value out of the sinogram so that it could be
estimated (`real_scan_validation.py:271-273`).  The far-slice job applied candidate rotations
to those scans with the bilinear kernel and judged them by the direct reconstruction, which has
no regularization, so a geometry error changes it directly.  The vendor's 0.167 degrees gave
the sharpest far slices on both scans (`real_scan_rotation_recon.md`).  This result validates
the sign and the approximate size of the correction against a physical detector.  It does not
locate the optimum between candidates, so it bounds the correction's scale far more loosely
than the synthetic recoveries bound the convention (`real_scan_rotation_recon.md`).  The same
result shows the estimator's zero point to be wrong on that object.  The far-slice job shares
the bilinear kernel deliberately, because it judges the reconstruction the correction would
actually produce.

The doubt therefore changes how two things are read, and it changes no result.  The synthetic
rotation tables are consistency and interpolation-bias measurements, not independent
validations, and the independent validation is the far-slice job.  One record also needs a
correction.  The limits section of `real_scan_rotation_check.md` says the estimator applies
each candidate by bilinear resampling and so shares one kernel with the added rotation.  At the
commit that job ran, the estimator's resampling is cubic (`git show
4781600:mbirtorch/preprocess/geometry_calibration.py`, line 1298).  The added-rotation test
therefore crossed two implementations, which strengthens it, and the record's sentence should
be corrected.  One related asymmetry belongs here.  Only the rotation search resamples
cubically; `conjugate_difference` and the `det_rotation` argument of the offset estimator
rotate through `reduce_sinogram` with the bilinear kernel (`geometry_calibration.py:443`,
`:1018`).

## What is solid today

**The offset estimator.**  The offset estimator is accurate on full rotations, on synthetic and
on real data.  Synthetic recovery is within 0.023 channels over offsets of -3.5 to 3.5 at a 20
degree fan (`conjugate_offset_recovery.md`).  On the cluster it is within 0.005 channels on a
512-channel detector and within 0.001 on a 1024-channel one (`calibration_512_gautschi.md`).
On four real scans the roll errors were at most 0.048 channels and the robustness differences
at most 0.069, against gates of 0.1 (`real_scan_validation.md`).  Vendor agreement is within
0.074 channels at the module's own rotation and within 0.18 channels at the far-slice job's
rotation, against a gate of 0.25 (`real_scan_validation.md`, `increment_6_findings.md`).
LEAP's `find_centerCol` agrees with the module to 0.001 channels on the Zeiss scan and reads
0.21 to 0.98 channels higher on the NSI scans (`real_scan_validation.md`).  The cause of that
gap has not been tested, and decision 7 below takes it up.

**The rotation estimator.**  The rotation estimator's response is right and its zero point
depends on the object.  Known rotations added to the real sinograms came back with slopes of
-1.00 to -1.01 and residuals of 0.006 degrees or less (`real_scan_rotation_check.md`).  On the
artifact phantom without metal the zero point is wrong by about 0.12 degrees.  On the metal
scan it is wrong by about 0.02 degrees, and that figure rests on two candidates the far-slice
measures separate by only a few percent (`real_scan_rotation_recon.md`).  The estimator's own
two readings on the metal scan, 0.149 degrees directly and 0.193 degrees as the added-rotation
intercept, differ by 0.044 degrees (`real_scan_rotation_check.md`).  The construction explains
the zero-point error.  A row band of 5 to 11 rows holds almost no rotation signal when the
object's structure runs along the rows (`increment_6_findings.md`).  LEAP's `estimate_tilt`
found nothing usable on these scans.  Its cost is monotone over the range where the rotation
lies on the NSI scans, with a 128-row band or with the full detector height, so it returns a
bound or a drift (`real_scan_leap_tilt.md`).  The band-slope method, a fit of per-band offset
estimates against band height, followed added rotations with slopes of only 0.70 to 0.80, and
it has no synthetic control at this detector size (`real_scan_rotation_check.md`).

**The direction check.**  The direction check is right on most scans and cannot be trusted at
one setting.  It answered right on three of four real scans, and its margin warning marked the
one wrong answer (`real_scan_validation.md`).  The warning marks uncertainty rather than error,
because it also fired on a correct answer at another setting (`real_scan_followup.md`).  The
wrong answer came from scoring at the pixel scale.  At the module's default filter widths, on
the 200-view scan reduced at a bin factor of 4, the high-pass filter kept 0.86 percent of the
reduced sinogram's energy (`real_scan_followup.md`).  The two scans measured respond to wider
filters in opposite directions, so no single setting serves both, and a sound rule requires
agreement across settings (`real_scan_followup.md`).

**The short scan and the residual score.**  A real short scan exists, and no automatic estimate
works on it yet.  `z62` covers 218 degrees and the conjugate-view method refuses it; the staged
Increment 6 error message names `parameter_sweep` as the manual path (`real_scan_followup.md`,
`increment_6_findings.md`).  On `z62` the module's direct-residual score has its minimum in a
plausible place.  Two channels away the score rises only 1.5 to 1.8 percent, which is about
thirty times less than on the synthetic short scan, so the score cannot be searched there at
the default filter widths (`real_scan_followup.md`).  The synthetic probe behind the
residual-method proposal gave ratios of 7.4 to 15.9 on full rotations and helical scans with
the whole axial extent kept (`residual_score_probe.md`).  The real measurement makes the
synthetic ratio a hypothesis to test, not a design input, until the filter widths are swept.

**Scope limits that stand.**  Three limits apply to all of the above.  Every entry point
refuses a sinogram in the divided device form and runs on the host in numpy; the two largest
real-scan jobs peaked near 50 GB of host memory (`real_scan_validation.md`,
`real_scan_followup.md`).  Helical and multiaxis scans have no automatic estimator, and
`parameter_sweep` is their manual path.  Three of the four validated real scans are one object,
so the real-data conclusions rest on two objects and two scanners.

## The case for and against each remaining piece of work

Decisions 1 to 4 weigh new work.  Decisions 5 to 7 are smaller: a set of plan edits, a recorded
deferral, and one ablation.

**Decision 1: the rotation zero point.**  The rotation estimate cannot be trusted without a
vendor value, and Greg decides the response.  Three facts argue for acting.  The
Zeiss readers supply no vendor tilt.  `z62` in particular has no vendor tilt and no module
estimate, and the only tilt reading on it, LEAP's 0.077 degrees, stands alone
(`real_scan_followup.md`).
The demo and the documented workflow both exercise the estimator.  Two facts argue against
acting.  The NSI reader supplies a vendor tilt, and the one Zeiss scan measured shows a
rotation below 0.02 degrees by two methods (`real_scan_followup.md`).  The docs already tell
users to prefer a vendor value.  Three remedies differ in cost:

- A failure detector.  The estimator reports its answer as undecided when the score minimum is
  shallow or when several band and filter settings disagree, in the same way the
  direction-check rule of decision 3 works.  Estimate: hours.  This makes the failure visible
  without building a new estimator.
- The far-slice sharpness sweep as the estimator.  The far-slice job's blurred measure ranked
  the true rotation first on every far slice of both scans, and one slice at four rotations
  took 3 to 20 seconds on the H100 (`real_scan_rotation_recon.md`).  Estimate: two days, with a
  synthetic and a real check.
- A wider comparison band with a cone-angle correction.  Two related methods failed on these
  scans, which is a caution rather than a disproof.  The band-slope fit read about three
  quarters of an added rotation, with no synthetic control at this detector size, and LEAP over
  the full height found nothing while also lacking the cone correction this remedy would add
  (`real_scan_rotation_check.md`, `real_scan_leap_tilt.md`).  This is the riskiest of the
  three.

**Decision 2: the short-scan method, which is Increment 3 as redefined.**  Whether to build the
conjugate-view method for short scans should be decided on the depot inventory's evidence.
Four facts argue for building it.  A real short scan exists.  The conjugate method compares
data with data, and the one automatic alternative measured on `z62` cannot be searched there
(`real_scan_followup.md`).  The plan's risk section calls the scan class common in industry.
Two agreeing methods sit an eighth of a channel from the `z62` vendor offset, so an independent
estimate would add evidence on which value is right (`real_scan_followup.md`).  Four facts
argue against.  `z62` is the only short scan named.  Its sweep already confirms the vendor
value against candidates half a channel away (`real_scan_followup.md`).  The accuracy of
one-sided pairing is unmeasured.  The work also needs the redundancy weighting
(`increment_6_findings.md`).  Estimate: three days, plus one for the weighting.  Increment 2's
findings name the scope: a mask per pair in the score, a changed coverage rule, and a remedy
for one-sided pairs (`increment_2_findings.md`).

If the method is built, five gates would test the risks the records name:

- recovery of a known offset on a synthetic short scan to 0.1 channels, with an off-axis
  phantom, because the tenfold one-sided error appeared on the off-axis rod and not on the
  centered phantom (`increment_2_findings.md`, `conjugate_offset_recovery.md`);
- a measurement that chooses between the two one-sided remedies, both-direction scoring and
  cubic partner interpolation (`increment_2_findings.md`);
- a regression gate, in which the per-pair masked score reproduces the current estimator on a
  full rotation to within the search tolerance;
- a test of the new coverage rule on three inputs: a full rotation, `z62`, and a scan below the
  rule's threshold;
- on `z62`, a roll test within 0.1 channels, with the vendor comparison reported as a sanity
  check only, because the vendor value is itself in dispute by an eighth of a channel
  (`real_scan_followup.md`).

**Decision 3: the direction-check rule.**  The check needs a multi-setting rule before its
answer can be trusted, and the rule's design needs one choice settled.  Three facts argue for
building it.  The check gave a wrong answer at its default settings on a real scan.  The
failure mode is understood.  The bin-factor route is cheap, at 0.35 seconds for a bin factor of
8 against 11 seconds at 2 on the 200-view scan (`real_scan_followup.md`).  Three facts argue
for caution.  The filter-width route costs more than a default run on the Zeiss scan, at 214
seconds for four widths against 72 (`real_scan_followup.md`).  The job varied the bin factor
and the filter widths one at a time, so which setting the rule should vary is unsettled.  The
evidence is two scans that differ in view count, view stride, and fan angle.  Estimate: a day
with its test.  The depot inventory would widen this evidence first.

**Decision 4: the driver, which is Increment 5 reduced.**  The driver can be built once the
zero-point decision settles what it should do with the rotation.  Three facts argue for it.
The real-scan gates passed, which is the condition the evaluation set out.  The driver
automates the three-step sequence that was run by hand on every scan.  It is fifty to a hundred
lines with its tests (`increment_3_evaluation.md`).  One fact argues for waiting.  Until
decision 1 is made, the driver would automate a step the docs tell users to distrust, so it
must at least prefer a vendor tilt when the reader supplies one.  Estimate: a day, after decision 1.

**Decision 5: the plan corrections.**  The plan's working copy needs four edits.  The Increment
2 line reads "COMPLETE 2026-08-04", and the date should be 2026-09-04
(`increment_3_evaluation.md`).  The corrections list has no entry for the order of work Greg
chose on 2026-09-04, and none recording the deferral of Increment 4
(`increment_6_findings.md`, `increment_3_evaluation.md`).  The v3 change list still says the
estimators handle an offset scan, and the module's docstring says an offset scan is not served
(`increment_3_evaluation.md`).  The search's evaluation-count figure of about fifteen, and the
run-time estimates resting on it, should be revised to the measured counts below.  Estimate:
minutes, on approval.

**Decision 6: the residual method, which is Increment 4.**  The residual method stays deferred.
The direct-residual score recovered a synthetic helical offset to 0.010 channels with the whole
axial extent kept (`residual_score_probe.md`).  On the real short scan the same score is about
thirty times shallower than its synthetic counterpart, so the synthetic contrast does not
transfer, and a filter-width sweep must precede any design (`real_scan_followup.md`).  No
helical or multiaxis scan is named.  Deferring costs nothing now, and the plan edit that
records the deferral is part of decision 5.

**Decision 7: the LEAP offset gap.**  The gap is the largest unexplained number in the record,
and it blocks nothing.  LEAP's `find_centerCol` reads 0.21 to 0.98 channels above the module on
the NSI scans, while the module stays within 0.08 channels of the vendor there
(`real_scan_validation.md`).  The held-out tilt was tested as a cause and does not fit.  That
tilt shifts LEAP's 128-row band by at most 0.19 channels, and the gap varies fivefold across
three scans carrying the same tilt (`real_scan_validation.md`).  Two cheap ablations remain:
run LEAP on the sinogram with the vendor tilt applied, and vary the row band LEAP is given.
Estimate: an hour inside any future cluster job.

## The plan's Increment 3 gates, resolved

The plan's Increment 3 text no longer describes work to be done, and its four gates resolve as
follows.  The derivative-filter method was dropped by Greg's decision of 2026-09-04, recorded
in the plan's corrections list.  Its two gates therefore lapse: the noise-floor factor, and the
unchanged ramp results.

The search gate is met on accuracy and missed on evaluation count.  The coarse pass and the
golden-section polish exist in `_search_minimum`.  They recover offsets to within 0.005
channels on a 512-channel detector and within 0.001 on a 1024-channel one
(`calibration_512_gautschi.md`).  One case failed there.  A true offset of 7.5 channels sat
outside the fixed search window, and the moving window that fixed it has been verified only on
a 64-channel detector (`calibration_512_gautschi.md`, `increment_3_evaluation.md`).  Each
search spends 24 score evaluations plus 11 more choosing the trimmed pairs, and the cone-beam
second pass doubles those counts (`increment_2_findings.md`, `real_scan_validation.md`).  The
plan's figure of about fifteen evaluations is therefore wrong, and decision 5 revises it.

The wall-time gate produced its measurement and not its comparison.  At 512 channels the offset
estimator takes 0.3 to 0.7 seconds and the rotation estimator 2.1 to 2.8 seconds on the H100
node (`calibration_512_gautschi.md`).  The reconstruction wall time the gate pairs those with
was not recorded, and the run-time estimates the comparison would check rest on the
fifteen-evaluation figure.
