## Environment

The prototype lives in `plans/experiments/geometry_viewer/`.  It needs a Python
environment with numpy, matplotlib, pytest, gradio, and mbirtorch importable; the
browser test also needs `playwright` with its Chromium.  The complete test suite,
226 tests, runs with

    cd plans/experiments/geometry_viewer
    MPLBACKEND=Agg python -m pytest -q test_geometry_scene.py test_geometry_viewer.py \
        test_geometry_interaction.py test_geometry_defaults.py test_web_app.py

and must pass before anything is staged.  `gv_show_example.py` opens the desktop viewer
on any of six geometries; `web/app.py` runs the Gradio app locally with `python app.py`.

## Part 2: finish the geometry viewer plan

Then return to `geometry_viewer_plan.md` and work its open items and remaining
increments, each with a review stop.

Open items, in the order to take them:

- The fit statement projects the corners of the reconstruction box, and the
  automatic box always overshoots the detector at its corners, so every
  automatically sized cone scan reads "volume fits det: no".  When the
  region-of-reconstruction mask is on, `volume_fits_detector` should project the
  cylinder's outline instead, and the detector-face panel should draw that outline.
  For a helical scan the statement should compare the volume's z extent with the
  detector's swept coverage, and count the views in which the volume leaves the
  detector, rather than demand that every view contain it.
- The comparison section of the text panel runs out of room when two geometries
  differ in many parameters; give it a panel or a window of its own.
- The web page fixes the 3D camera; two number fields for `elevation_deg` and
  `azimuth_deg` would give it what the mouse gives the desktop.
- The multiaxis side view's source marker covers part of the `recon_slice_offset`
  label.

Increment 5, the data overlays: a sinogram view painted onto the detector face for
the current view, in the same row and channel orientation as the grid (row 0 at the
top), checked by painting a synthetic sinogram with one bright pixel at a known row
and channel; and a reconstruction or phantom drawn as a silhouette in the volume box.

Increment 6, the port into mbirtorch: move `geometry_scene.py` and
`geometry_viewer.py` into the `mbirtorch` package following the slice viewer's
pattern (`mbirtorch/viewer.py` with its thin wrapper in `mbirtorch/view_utils.py`);
expose `mbirtorch.geometry_viewer(ct_model, ...)` beside `mbirtorch.slice_viewer`,
with the same nonblocking registry behavior; move the tests; add a demo, a docs page
under `docs/source/`, and a row in `plans/API_specification.md` under "Viewing".  Two
docstrings in mbirtorch disagree with the measured conventions and should be fixed in
the same change: the `TranslationModel` docstring says a positive z translation
shifts the object up, and the measurement says it moves the object toward -z; the
`MultiAxisParallelModel` docstring should define the elevation as the angle at
which the source, on the +y side, looks up at the object, so that a positive
elevation puts the source below the xy plane.  `geometry_defaults.py` is a copy of
mbirtorch's automatic-geometry rules made for the web page; in the port, the web
page can keep using it, and its test against the real models is what keeps the copy
honest.

Decisions already made, not to be reopened without Greg: matplotlib is the frontend;
the object frame stays right-handed with +z along the slice index and the drawing
puts -z at the top; the multiaxis source is on the +y side; the entry point is the
function `geometry_viewer`, not a model method; PNG figures are not tracked in
`mbirtorch_plans`, except the Space tile and the logo copy under `web/`.
